Nature.

Step outside and discover the amazing living world around you. Learn why leaves change colour, how seeds grow, what minibeasts need and how habitats support wildlife.

What is nature?

Nature includes all the living things and natural places around us: trees, flowers, grasses, insects, birds, mammals, rivers, soil and much more. Every living thing has a place in its environment.

Plants: Plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to make their own food. Roots take in water, stems support the plant and transport water, and leaves collect sunlight.

Habitats: A habitat is the place where a plant or animal lives. It provides things it needs, such as food, water, shelter and space.

Food chains: Plants are often producers because they make their own food. Animals get energy by eating plants or other animals. This connects living things together.

Seasons: The amount of daylight and the weather change through the year. Many trees lose their leaves in autumn, and plants and animals respond to seasonal changes in different ways.

Explore nature

LEARN

Leaves and trees

Leaves come in many shapes and sizes. Their veins form transport routes, while their broad surfaces help collect sunlight. Trees can be identified by clues such as leaf shape, bark and seeds.

DISCOVER

Seeds and plant growth

A seed contains a tiny young plant. When it has the right conditions, it germinates: a root grows down and a shoot grows upwards. Plants need light, water, air and suitable warmth to grow.

INVESTIGATE

Minibeasts and habitats

Worms, woodlice, beetles and other small creatures live in different microhabitats. Damp, dark places can provide shelter and help prevent some creatures from drying out.

UNDERSTAND

Food chains and ecosystems

A food chain shows how energy passes between living things. If one part of a habitat changes, it can affect other plants and animals that depend on it.

Fun nature experiments

Choose an experiment, gather the materials and make a prediction before you begin.

01 · LEAF ART

Leaf vein detective

Discover the branching patterns inside a leaf.

What to do
  1. Find a few fallen leaves with different shapes.
  2. Place one under a sheet of paper, vein-side up.
  3. Rub the side of a crayon gently over the paper.
  4. Compare the vein patterns you reveal.
Think about: Why might leaves need veins?
02 · COLOUR CHANGE

Autumn leaf colour hunt

Make a colourful chart of fallen leaves.

What to do
  1. Collect fallen leaves of different colours.
  2. Sort them into green, yellow, orange, red and brown groups.
  3. Count each group and make a tally chart.
  4. Repeat in a different place and compare.
Think about: Why do many leaves change colour in autumn?
03 · WATER TEST

Do leaves repel water?

Find out how water behaves on different leaf surfaces.

What to do
  1. Lay several fallen leaves on a tray.
  2. Use a teaspoon to place one small drop of water on each.
  3. Watch whether the water spreads, beads up or rolls away.
  4. Compare smooth, waxy and rough leaves.
Think about: Could a water-repellent surface help a leaf?
04 · SEED SCIENCE

Grow a bean in a jar

Watch a seed wake up and begin to grow.

What to do
  1. Line a clear jar with damp kitchen paper.
  2. Place a bean between the paper and the glass.
  3. Keep the paper damp, not flooded.
  4. Check each day and draw the root and shoot as they appear.
Think about: Which part grows first? Wash hands after handling seeds.
05 · PLANT TRANSPORT

Rainbow celery

See how coloured water travels through a plant.

What to do
  1. Ask an adult to put water and food colouring in a clear cup.
  2. Place a celery stalk with leaves in the coloured water.
  3. Look at the stalk and leaf veins after a few hours and the next day.
  4. Draw where the colour has travelled.
Think about: What does this show about how plants move water? Do not eat the celery.
06 · FLOATING FUN

Leaf boat challenge

Design a tiny boat using fallen leaves.

What to do
  1. Choose a sturdy fallen leaf.
  2. Float it in a shallow tray of water.
  3. Test whether it can carry one small pebble, then two.
  4. Try a different leaf shape and compare.
Think about: Which leaf carried the most weight before sinking?
07 · MINIBEAST SAFARI

Microhabitat explorer

Find out where tiny creatures might live.

What to do
  1. With an adult, observe a patch of grass, a hedge and the ground near a tree.
  2. Look carefully without picking up or disturbing creatures.
  3. Record what you see and where you saw it.
  4. Compare which place had the most signs of life.
Think about: Why might a woodlouse prefer a damp, shady place?
08 · NATURE DETECTIVE

Bark pattern printing

Make a picture of tree bark textures.

What to do
  1. Ask an adult to help choose a safe, sturdy tree.
  2. Hold paper gently against the bark without damaging it.
  3. Rub a crayon sideways over the paper.
  4. Compare the patterns from different trees.
Think about: Can bark patterns help you tell trees apart? Never peel or damage bark.
09 · SOIL SCIENCE

Make a soil shake jar

See how soil can contain different-sized particles.

What to do
  1. With an adult, put a small amount of soil and water in a sealed clear jar.
  2. Shake gently, then leave it to settle.
  3. Watch the heavier particles settle first and finer material later.
  4. Draw the layers you can see.
Think about: Why do different particles settle at different speeds? Do not open or taste the jar contents; wash hands.
10 · WEATHER WATCH

Make a wind streamer

Use a simple streamer to observe wind direction.

What to do
  1. Tie a few strips of lightweight paper to a pencil or stick.
  2. Hold it outdoors in an open, safe place with an adult.
  3. Notice which way the strips blow.
  4. Repeat at different times and record what changes.
Think about: Is the wind always blowing from the same direction?
11 · NATURE MATHS

Build a leaf size graph

Turn your leaf measurements into a graph.

What to do
  1. Measure the length of five fallen leaves.
  2. Write the measurements in a table.
  3. Draw a bar chart showing each leaf's length.
  4. Find the longest, shortest and average length.
Think about: Which leaf was closest to the average?
12 · SEASON SPOTTER

Four-week nature diary

Watch one outdoor spot change over time.

What to do
  1. Choose a safe place you can revisit with an adult.
  2. Draw or photograph the same view once a week.
  3. Record changes in leaves, flowers, weather and wildlife.
  4. Compare your first and last observations.
Think about: Which changes happened slowly and which happened quickly?

Outdoor activities

Head outside with an adult, explore carefully and record what you discover. Leave plants, animals and habitats undisturbed.

OUTDOOR CHALLENGE 01

Nature scavenger hunt

Find natural patterns and objects without picking or damaging anything.

Open checklist
  • A leaf with a jagged edge
  • A feather on the ground
  • Three different natural colours
  • A seed or fallen seed pod
  • Evidence of an animal
  • A plant growing in a surprising place
Extra challenge: Sketch your discoveries.
OUTDOOR CHALLENGE 02

Build a mini nature trail

Plan a short route with interesting stopping points.

Open activity
  1. Choose a safe route with an adult.
  2. Pick four places to stop and observe.
  3. At each stop, write down one thing you notice.
  4. Make a simple map and add your discoveries.
OUTDOOR CHALLENGE 03

Birdwatching tracker

Watch quietly and record the birds you spot.

Open activity
  1. Find a safe place to sit with an adult.
  2. Watch for ten minutes without disturbing wildlife.
  3. Record bird colours, sizes, calls and behaviour.
  4. Compare your notes on another day.
OUTDOOR CHALLENGE 04

Measure a tree

Use maths to investigate trees in your local area.

Open activity
  1. Choose a tree you can safely reach with an adult.
  2. Measure around its trunk using a tape measure.
  3. Estimate its height by comparing it with a known object.
  4. Repeat for two other trees and compare.
OUTDOOR CHALLENGE 05

Cloud and weather diary

Become a weather watcher for a week.

Open activity
  1. Look at the sky at the same time each day.
  2. Draw the clouds and note the weather.
  3. Record whether it is windy, sunny, cloudy or rainy.
  4. Compare the week's observations.
OUTDOOR CHALLENGE 06

Design a wildlife-friendly space

Plan a small way to help nature.

Open activity
  1. Look at an outdoor space with an adult.
  2. Draw a plan with shelter, water and native plants.
  3. Explain which wildlife might benefit.
  4. Ask an adult before changing or planting anything.

Become a nature scientist

For every experiment, make a prediction, follow the steps, record what happened and explain what you discovered. Always leave nature as you found it: only use fallen leaves, never taste wild plants or fungi, and explore outdoors with an adult.
