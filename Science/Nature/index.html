Your nature mission

You do not need special equipment. Most investigations use leaves and things you can find outdoors. Observe carefully, compare results and write down what you notice.

Nature safety: Ask an adult before collecting anything. Do not eat plants, berries or fungi. Wash your hands after outdoor activities, avoid touching unknown insects, and leave living plants and animals where you find them. Only collect fallen leaves.

18 nature experiments & activities

Choose an activity and open the instructions. Use the filters to find leaf investigations or other nature challenges.

01 · LEAF INVESTIGATION

Leaf rubbing art

Reveal the shapes and veins hidden on a leaf.

Materials & method
  • Fallen leaves, paper and wax crayons.
  1. Place a leaf vein-side up under paper.
  2. Rub the crayon sideways over the paper.
  3. Compare the patterns from different leaves.
Investigate: Which leaf makes the clearest pattern?
02 · LEAF SCIENCE

Make a leaf collection

Sort fallen leaves by shape, size and colour.

Materials & method
  • Fallen leaves, paper and pencil.
  1. Find 6–10 different fallen leaves.
  2. Sort them into groups by shape or edge.
  3. Draw and label each group.
Investigate: Can you make more than one sensible sorting system?
03 · LEAF MEASUREMENT

Which leaf is biggest?

Measure leaves and compare their sizes.

Materials & method
  • Fallen leaves, ruler and notebook.
  1. Measure the length and widest point of each leaf.
  2. Record the results in a table.
  3. Order the leaves from smallest to largest.
Investigate: Is the longest leaf also the widest?
04 · LEAF PRINTING

Leaf printing

Use leaf textures to create nature prints.

Materials & method
  • Fallen leaves, washable paint, paper and a brush.
  1. Brush a thin layer of paint onto one side of a leaf.
  2. Press it gently onto paper.
  3. Lift it carefully and compare the prints.
Investigate: Which side shows the strongest vein pattern?
05 · LEAF EXPERIMENT

Do leaves let water roll off?

Compare how water behaves on different leaf surfaces.

Materials & method
  • Fallen leaves, water dropper or teaspoon and tray.
  1. Lay each leaf on the tray.
  2. Add the same small drop of water to each.
  3. Watch whether the drop spreads, sits or rolls.
Investigate: Do shiny and dull leaves behave differently? Dry the leaves afterwards.
06 · LEAF OBSERVATION

Discover leaf veins

Look closely at the tiny branching lines in leaves.

Materials & method
  • Fallen leaves and a magnifying glass.
  1. Look at the main vein running through a leaf.
  2. Trace the smaller veins branching from it.
  3. Draw the pattern you see.
Investigate: Do all leaves have the same vein pattern?
07 · LEAF CLASSIFICATION

Match leaves to trees

Become a tree detective using leaf clues.

Materials & method
  • Fallen leaves, a local tree guide or adult helper.
  1. Choose leaves from the ground near different trees.
  2. Notice their shape, edge and arrangement of veins.
  3. Use a trusted guide to identify possible trees.
Investigate: Which clues helped you most? Do not pull leaves from trees.
08 · LEAF COLOURS

Autumn colour detective

Track the different colours found in fallen leaves.

Materials & method
  • Fallen leaves, colour pencils and notebook.
  1. Find leaves with different colours.
  2. Make a colour chart and draw one example of each.
  3. Repeat on another day or in another place.
Investigate: Which colours are most common in your collection?
09 · LEAF FLOAT TEST

Do leaves float?

Compare leaves in water without damaging plants.

Materials & method
  • A bowl of water and a few fallen leaves.
  1. Predict which leaves will float.
  2. Place them gently on the water.
  3. Record what happens, then remove and dry them.
Investigate: Does leaf size or shape seem to make a difference?
10 · PLANT GROWTH

Watch a seed germinate

Observe how a seed begins to grow.

Materials & method
  • A bean seed, clear jar, kitchen paper and water.
  1. Dampen the paper and place it inside the jar.
  2. Slide the bean between the paper and jar.
  3. Keep it damp, not soaked, and observe daily.
  4. Draw changes as the root and shoot appear.
Investigate: What appears first? Ask an adult about planting the seed afterwards.
11 · PLANT NEEDS

What do plants need?

Plan a fair test about one condition plants need.

Materials & method
  • Two similar potted seedlings and adult help.
  1. Choose one factor to investigate, such as light.
  2. Keep water, pot and plant type the same.
  3. Change only the chosen factor and observe over time.
Investigate: Why must you change only one factor? Keep plants safe and do not leave them without water for long.
12 · WATER MOVEMENT

Colour travels up a stem

See how water moves through a plant stem.

Materials & method
  • A pale celery stalk with leaves, water, food colouring and a clear cup.
  1. Ask an adult to prepare a cup with water and a few drops of food colouring.
  2. Place the celery stalk in the cup.
  3. Check the stem and leaf veins after a few hours and again the next day.
Investigate: Where can you see the colour? Do not eat the celery used in the experiment.
13 · HABITAT HUNT

Mini habitat survey

Find out what lives in different outdoor places.

Materials & method
  • Notebook, pencil and magnifying glass.
  1. Observe a patch of grass, a hedge and a tree base.
  2. Look for signs of life without disturbing anything.
  3. Record plants, insects and other clues you spot.
Investigate: Which place had the most different signs of life?
14 · WEATHER WATCH

Make a nature weather diary

Connect changing weather with what you see outdoors.

Materials & method
  • Notebook and pencil.
  1. At the same time each day, record cloud, wind and temperature feel.
  2. Draw or describe what leaves and plants are doing.
  3. Compare your notes after one week.
Investigate: What changed after windy, sunny or rainy days?
15 · NATURE PATTERNS

Find patterns in nature

Spot spirals, stripes, branching and repeating shapes.

Materials & method
  • Notebook or camera with adult permission.
  1. Look for patterns in leaves, bark, flowers and shells.
  2. Sketch three patterns.
  3. Describe what repeats or branches.
Investigate: Which patterns appear in more than one natural object?
16 · SOIL STUDY

Compare soil textures

Observe how soil looks and feels in different places.

Materials & method
  • Small samples of soil from places where collecting is allowed, tray and spoon.
  1. Ask an adult to help collect tiny samples.
  2. Compare colour, grain size and how crumbly each sample is.
  3. Return samples to the ground after observing.
Investigate: Are the samples the same? Do not taste or inhale soil; wash hands afterwards.
17 · TREE STUDY

Measure a tree without climbing

Estimate and compare the size of trees safely.

Materials & method
  • String, ruler and notebook with adult help.
  1. Wrap string around the trunk at a comfortable height, without pulling tight.
  2. Measure the string length with a ruler.
  3. Compare the measurements of different trees.
Investigate: Which tree has the largest trunk measurement? Never climb trees or damage bark.
18 · SEASONAL CHANGE

Make a seasonal spot

Return to one outdoor place and see how it changes.

Materials & method
  • Notebook and pencil.
  1. Choose a place you can visit safely with an adult.
  2. Draw the same view once a week.
  3. Record changes in leaves, flowers, light and wildlife.
Investigate: What changes slowly, and what changes quickly?

Nature knowledge check

Try these four questions when you have finished exploring.

1. What do leaf veins help move around a leaf?
2. Which part of a seedling usually grows down first?
3. What should you collect for leaf activities?
4. What makes a fair test?

Investigator challenge

Pick one activity, write down your prediction, record what happened and explain what you learned. That is how real scientists work.
