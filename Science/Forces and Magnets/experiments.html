HANDS-ON INVESTIGATION

TIME TO EXPERIMENT

Get your magnets out and investigate forces for yourself. Then build a ramp, test toy cars on different surfaces and discover how friction changes how far they travel.

Experiment 1: Magnet forces

You will need: two bar magnets and a few small objects.

  1. Hold two magnets with their ends facing each other.
  2. Slowly bring the magnets closer together. What do you feel?
  3. Turn one magnet around and try again.
  4. Test which objects are attracted to a magnet. Try a paper clip, coin, pencil and piece of paper.
Think about it: Which poles attract? Which poles repel? Can a magnet pull an object without touching it?

Experiment 2: Ramp racers

You will need: a toy car, a board or sturdy ramp, books, tape measure and different surface materials.

  1. Make a gentle ramp by resting one end of a board securely on a stack of books.
  2. Choose one surface, such as smooth paper, a towel or carpet. Place it on the flat track after the ramp.
  3. Put the same car at the same starting line every time.
  4. Let go without pushing. Measure how far the car travels from the end of the ramp.
  5. Repeat three times on each surface and compare your results.

Ask an adult to help set up the ramp. Keep it low and stable, use a clear floor space, and keep the car away from stairs and people.

What is happening?

Friction is a force that acts between surfaces. Rougher surfaces usually create more friction, which can slow the car down sooner. Gravity pulls the car down the ramp and makes it speed up.

Be a scientist

Make a prediction before each test. Was your prediction correct? Which surface let the car travel furthest? What could you change to make the test fair?
