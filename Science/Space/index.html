EXPERIMENT 01 · MOON

Make a Moon crater

Find out why the Moon has round dents called impact craters.

See materials and instructionsYou need
  • A shallow tray
  • Flour and a little cocoa powder
  • A small, smooth ball or marble
  • A ruler and paper
Try it
  1. Put a layer of flour in the tray and lightly dust cocoa over it.
  2. Drop the ball gently from a measured height.
  3. Measure the crater width. Smooth the surface and repeat from a different height.
  4. Compare the craters. What changed when the ball was dropped from higher up?

Think: How might the size, speed and angle of an incoming space rock change a crater?

EXPERIMENT 02 · MOON PHASES

Model the Moon's phases

See why the Moon seems to change shape during the month.

See materials and instructionsYou need
  • A lamp or torch (the Sun)
  • A small ball on a stick (the Moon)
  • A dim room and an adult to help
Try it
  1. Place the lamp in one spot and switch it on. Never stare into the light.
  2. Hold the ball at arm's length between you and the lamp.
  3. Slowly turn on the spot, keeping the ball at arm's length.
  4. Watch how the lit part you can see changes as the ball moves around you.

Think: Does the Moon make its own light, or reflect sunlight?

Keep the lamp stable, avoid hot bulbs and never look directly into a bright light.
EXPERIMENT 03 · ROCKETS

Build a straw rocket

Investigate how changing a paper rocket affects how far it travels.

See materials and instructionsYou need
  • Paper and a drinking straw
  • Tape and scissors
  • A measuring tape
Try it
  1. Roll a small rectangle of paper around a pencil to make a loose tube.
  2. Tape the seam and fold one end closed to make the nose.
  3. Add small paper fins near the open end.
  4. Slide it over the straw and blow through the straw to launch it across a clear space.
  5. Change one thing at a time, such as fin size, and measure the distance.

Think: Which design travelled furthest? Was it a fair test?

Only launch paper rockets. Never aim at faces, people or animals. Use scissors with care and ask an adult for help.
EXPERIMENT 04 · ORBITS

Explore gravity and orbits

Use a simple model to think about how planets travel around the Sun.

See materials and instructionsYou need
  • A large sheet of paper
  • A pencil and string
  • A small object to mark the centre
Try it
  1. Mark the Sun near the centre of the paper.
  2. Ask an adult to help hold the string safely while you use it to draw a large circle around the Sun.
  3. Draw a second, smaller circle and label them as model orbits.
  4. Place planet labels on the paths and trace their journeys around the Sun.

Think: Why do planets keep travelling around the Sun instead of flying off in a straight line?

This is a drawing model, not a full demonstration of gravity.
